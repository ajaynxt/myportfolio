How much does a business website cost in India?

A clear guide to the factors that shape business website pricing in India, from scope and content to integrations and ongoing support.

← All insightsDiscuss a project ↗

There is no one price for every website

A five-page brochure website, a booking platform and a large e-commerce store are different products. A sensible estimate starts with the business goal and the features needed to achieve it—not an arbitrary page count.

1. Scope and page structure

List the pages customers actually need: home, about, services, product or category pages, portfolio, FAQs, contact and legal information. Unique layouts and complex content take more work than reusing a simple page template.

2. Design and content

Custom visual direction, photography, copywriting, illustrations and product data all affect effort. If the client supplies accurate content and usable images, the project may be simpler. If research, writing and image production are required, include them in the scope.

3. Features and integrations

Enquiry forms, WhatsApp flows, payments, booking, inventory, customer accounts, multilingual content and third-party APIs add implementation and testing work. Identify which features are essential for launch and which can wait.

4. Hosting, domain and maintenance

Domain registration, hosting, email, premium plugins, payment gateway charges and ongoing maintenance may be separate recurring costs. Clarify who owns each account and who pays renewals. Avoid promising lifetime hosting unless the costs and terms are genuinely covered.

5. A practical way to request a quote

  • Share the business type and primary goal.
  • List required pages and key features.
  • Provide examples of websites you like.
  • Confirm who supplies text, photos and product information.
  • Set a target launch date and review process.
  • Ask for deliverables, exclusions, payment stages and post-launch support in writing.

A transparent proposal should explain what is included, what is not included, the timeline assumptions and any third-party recurring costs. Compare proposals by scope and ownership—not just the headline price.
